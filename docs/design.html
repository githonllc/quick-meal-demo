Quick Meal Demo Design

A mobile web demo of a DoorDash concept. Built for university students in the Bay Area and across the US. A student opens Quick Meal, sets a budget, time and distance, and sees which meals fit. If nothing fits, the app shows the closest meals and a one-tap way to loosen the search.

Final designVersion 30Oct 1, 2026Concept mockups, not affiliated with DoorDash
Design in short
  • Screens: a thin Home, the Quick Meal list, a Filters sheet, a restaurant menu in budget view, and a price breakdown sheet.
  • Budget means the estimated all-in price of one meal: food, fees, tax and tip. This links the demo to our Week 3 top concept, Budget-First Ordering.
  • Results are meals, one per restaurant. Other meals that fit show as "+2 more under $20" and open the restaurant's menu.
  • Cuisine tabs across the top (All, Chinese, Indian …), one at a time. Filters: a budget slider plus time and distance steps.
  • No match is a designed state. It shows the closest meals, says how each one misses, and offers chips like "Budget up to $17 · 1 result".

Product decisions

How Quick Meal works

These hold for the real product, in any city.

P1

Entry point

Decision
First item in the Home category row, left of Deals. Red tile, white lightning icon, a NEW tag. The banner also opens Quick Meal. Other Home items show "Not part of this demo.", because category and restaurant pages are out of scope.
Why
The row scrolls sideways and only about five items fit on screen. First place is always visible. The rest of Home is white with plain line icons, as in the current DoorDash app, so one red tile stands out. It also matches the app icon.
P2

Filter controls

Layout
Two rows under the title. First the chip row: Filters · Budget · Time · Distance. Then the cuisine tabs, right above the list.
Why this order
Filters set the pool of meals that fit. A tab then shows one cuisine from that pool. Tabs sit next to the list they switch, the usual place for tabs. Changing a filter keeps the current tab.
Not DoorDash's order
DoorDash puts store-type tabs above filters because the tab decides which filters apply. Here the filters decide which meals fit, and the cuisine tab only splits them, so filters go first. With filters under the tabs, they would look like they belong to one tab, and users would wonder whether switching tabs keeps or resets them. With filters on top, it is clear they stay the same across every tab.
Budget
Slider for the maximum all-in price per meal. Range $10 to $40, in $1 steps. The value shows above the thumb: "Up to $20". The far right end reads "$40+ (any)" and means no budget filter.
Time
Steps: 15 · 20 · 30 · 45 min
Distance
Steps depend on the user's location. A dense area like downtown San Jose gets 0.5 · 1 · 2 · 3 mi. A spread-out suburb gets 1 · 2 · 3 · 5 mi.
Cuisine
Tabs: All · Chinese · Indian · Mexican · Pizza · Burgers · Healthy · Fast Food · Sushi. One at a time; All means any cuisine. The row scrolls sideways. A place can carry several tags, since Healthy and Fast Food are tags rather than cuisines, so one place can appear under more than one tab. Tabs show no counts. The Filters badge does not count the tab, because the selected tab is already visible. Applying filters keeps the current tab.
Sort
Best match · Lowest price · Fastest · Nearest. Only in the full sheet.
Chip tap
Each chip opens a small sheet for that one filter. Time and Distance apply at once when you pick a step. Budget shows a "Show results" button, so a drag does not reload the list on every dollar. A tab applies at once.
No live count
The button reads "Show results" and the search runs only when the user applies. A live count needs a request on every change, and a slow answer can show a stale number (for example "Show 0 results" that then shows meals). The count line on the list shows the number after applying.
Why
Budget is a number students already have in mind, often not a round one, so a slider fits it. Time and distance are rough choices, so a few steps are faster to tap. Cuisine answers "what am I in the mood for", which usually has one answer. A tab makes that one tap instead of three.
P3

The "Filters" chip

Decision
The first chip shows a sliders icon plus "Filters", with a badge for the active count. It opens the full Filters sheet, the same as the top-right icon.
Why
On most apps "All" means "no filter", so a chip named "All" that opens a sheet would surprise people. "Filters 2" also shows at a glance how many filters are on.
P4

Budget is the all-in price

Decision
Budget is the all-in price of one meal: the item, delivery fee, small-order fee, service fee, tax and tip. Every card and menu row shows it. Tap a price to see the breakdown (screen 6). Tax follows the restaurant's address. The tip is 15% by default.
Small-order fee
Some places charge a fee when the food costs less than a minimum. A single cheap meal often hits it, so it is part of the all-in price. It can make a cheaper menu item cost more: the $10.75 Tofu Rice Bowl comes to $19.48, more than the $12.00 Chicken Rice Bowl at $18.73.
Why
This is our Week 3 top concept, Budget-First Ordering. Students set a budget before they open the app but see the real total only at checkout. One interviewee put it as "the total jumped from like $13 to $24". A menu-price filter would repeat that problem.
Label
Cards say "Est. $18.73 all-in". The slider header says "Estimated all-in: food, fees, tax and tip". The breakdown is titled "Estimated all-in price". "Out-the-door price" is longer and comes from car buying.
Why "Est."
The price cannot be exact before checkout. The tip is the user's choice, the delivery and service fees can vary, and tax is estimated. "Est." keeps the number honest. It still shows cents, so it compares cleanly with the budget.

P5

Results are meals, one per place

Decision
Each result card is one meal. Dish photo on top. Then the dish name with its like rate, the restaurant with its star rating, and the est. all-in price, time, distance and "Fits".
Why meals
The budget is per meal, so the meal is what the student is choosing. An all-in price belongs to a dish, not to a place.
One per place
Each restaurant shows one lead meal. Its other meals that fit are named under it: "+2 more under $20: Tofu Rice Bowl, Spam Musubi Plate ›". The count reads "6 places have a meal that fits".
Why one
If every fitting meal were listed, one cheap place could fill the screen with five bowls. One per place keeps the list varied and quick to scan.
Lead meal
The card shows the first meal of that restaurant's menu in budget view (P6). One ordering rule serves both places. With Best match, Fastest or Nearest: most-liked first. With Lowest price: lowest est. all-in price first. With no budget set, the line reads "+4 more on the menu".
Other meals
Two ways, both one step. Read the names in the "+N more" line without leaving the list. Or tap the line or the card to open that restaurant's menu in budget view (P6).
Ratings
Two kinds, shown apart. The dish gets a thumbs-up rate and count, "88% (120)", next to its name. The restaurant gets stars, "4.6 ★ (800+)", next to its name. Different icon, different line, so they do not mix. The like rate helps pick the meal; the stars help trust the place.
P6

Restaurant menu in budget view

Decision
Tapping a card or its "+N more" line opens that restaurant's full menu. Nothing is highlighted. The meal from the card is simply the first item, because the card always shows the first meal of this view.
Layout
A budget bar on top: "Your budget: up to $20 est. all-in · Change". Change opens the budget sheet. Then "Under your budget (3)", in the current sort order: most-liked first, or cheapest first with Lowest price. Then "Over your budget (2)", smallest overage first, each tagged like "$0.54 over". Every row shows the dish, its like rate, the menu price and the est. all-in price.
Why one page
The student sees what fits and also what is just over. A $0.54 overage may be worth it, and a separate "fits only" list would hide it. It also keeps the restaurant's real menu as the single place to browse dishes.
No budget
The full menu in one list, in the same sort order, with an est. all-in price on every row and no sections.
Example
At Paseo Rice Bowl, the $11.50 Veggie Bowl is over a $20 budget while the $12.00 Chicken Rice Bowl fits. The small-order fee explains the gap, and tapping the price shows it.
P7

Time and distance work together

Decision
Delivery time and distance are separate filters. You can set one, both or neither. A place must pass every filter that is on. Setting one never clears the other.
Why
They measure different things. Delivery time is cooking time plus wait plus travel, so a close place can be slow and a farther one can be fast. Making them exclusive would quietly clear one filter when you set the other, and add a special rule to the page, the URL and the API.
Empty results
When both together are too tight, the no-match state shows the closest meals and one-tap relax chips.
P8

Filters are saved as the default

Decision
Quick Meal remembers the budget, time and distance you set and opens with them next time. A student sets "$20 · 30 min" once and does not set it again.
What saves
Only choices the user makes: "Show results" in the Filters sheet, or a value picked in a chip sheet. "Clear all" plus apply saves "no filters".
What does not
Relax chips on the no-match screen change the results for this visit only. The cuisine tab is never saved and goes back to All each visit.
Why
A student's budget is a fixed number. A one-time "Budget up to $17" should not quietly raise it for good. Cuisine is the mood of one meal; a saved "Pizza" would hide everything else next time.
Feedback
The first time filters are applied, a short toast says "Saved. Quick Meal will open with these filters." After that, the black chips are enough.
Edge cases
A link with filters in the URL wins over the saved default. A saved value outside the current range is moved to the nearest valid value.
Demo decisions

How this demo is built

These only shape the demo. They are not product decisions.

D1

Screen scope

Decision
Home (thin) → Quick Meal list → Filters sheet (plus a small single-filter sheet per chip) → Restaurant menu in budget view → Price breakdown sheet.
Home
Clickable but shallow. Category row, one banner, cuisine chips, a "Near campus" row. Search shows a toast: "Search is not part of this demo."
Not built
Cart, checkout, search and account. "Add to cart" shows a toast: "Cart is not part of this demo."
Why
The demo is about choosing a meal on a budget. The menu page shows the Week 3 concept in full: all-in prices on the list and the menu, with what breaks the budget marked.
D2

Fixed location and time

Decision
The location is fixed at San José State University in downtown San Jose, CA. "Now" is the only time. Closed places never appear. The page uses the dense-area distance steps. Filter steps, the slider range and the cuisine tabs are fixed in the page.
Why
A fixed location gives the same results in every run, so the demo script and the acceptance criteria stay stable. Asking for the phone's location would add a permission prompt during a live demo and change the results by where the presenter stands.
D3

Mock data, branding and photos

Decision
Realistic mock data with made-up restaurant names near campus. DoorDash-like red and layout, but no DoorDash logo. Footer line: "Concept demo for a CMU course. Not affiliated with DoorDash."
Realism
Menu prices, delivery fees and wait times follow what students see near the campus. The cuisine mix matches the area. A few places are "close but slow" or "far but fast", such as 4th St. Noodle Bar (0.3 mi, 19 min) and Santa Clara Taco Co. (0.9 mi, 16 min), so time and distance filters both matter.
Photos
About 15 to 20 free food photos (Unsplash or Pexels license), one per dish type such as bowl, noodles, burger or taco, shared by similar dishes. Compressed and bundled. Fallback: colored tiles with the dish type.
Why
DoorDash's US consumer terms, section 5 (p) to (r), ban collecting its text, photos or data by automated means or by "systematic retrieval", and its photos belong to DoorDash or the restaurants. The demo also lives on a public URL, where real store names next to made-up ratings, prices and wait times would show false facts about real businesses.
D4

Fee and tax values

Service fee
15%. This matches DoorDash's published California rate for orders without DashPass (5% with DashPass). The demo prices every meal as a non-member, the case after a student's free DashPass year ends.
Tax
One assumed rate of 9.5% for every place, stored per restaurant so the formula already works per address.
Small-order fee
$2.50 when the food costs less than $12. Assumed value.
Tip
15%, the default the user would see.
Rounding
All math in integer cents, each line rounded half up, so every breakdown adds up to the cent.
D5

Build choices

Saved filters
Stored in the phone's browser (local storage). The demo has no login, so a server copy would still be tied to one browser. If the browser blocks storage (some private modes), Quick Meal opens with no saved filters and shows no error.
Budget slider
A native range input with custom styling. It works with touch, mouse and keyboard, and screen readers read its value.
No-match results

When nothing matches

The API always returns three lists. The page picks the layout from them, so there is no special code path for "empty".

GET /api/quick-meal/search?budget=15&time=15&distance=0.5

{
  "filters": { "budget": 15, "time": 15, "distance": 0.5, "cuisine": null, "sort": "best" },
  "exact": [],
  "near": [
    { "restaurantId": "taylor-st-dumplings", "restaurant": "Taylor St. Dumplings",
      "dish": "Pork Dumplings (12)", "allIn": 16.32, "etaMin": 14, "distanceMi": 0.4,
      "miss": [ { "filter": "budget", "over": 1.32, "label": "$1.32 over budget" } ] },
    { "restaurantId": "fourth-st-noodle-bar", "dish": "Wonton Soup",
      "miss": [ { "filter": "time", "over": 4, "label": "4 min slower" } ] }
  ],
  "relax": [
    { "filter": "budget", "to": 17, "count": 1, "label": "Budget up to $17 · 1 result" },
    { "filter": "time",   "to": 20, "count": 1, "label": "Time up to 20 min · 1 result" }
  ]
}
  • Exact. One lead meal per open place that passes every active filter.
  • Near. Only filled when exact is empty. For each open place, take its meal that misses least. Score the miss: budget overshoot ÷ $5, time overshoot ÷ 10 min, distance overshoot ÷ 1 mi, then add them up. Show the 5 lowest scores. Each card lists what it misses in plain words.
  • Cuisine stays fixed. Near meals only miss on budget, time or distance, never on cuisine. A student who picked Indian never sees a burger as "close". If no place of that cuisine is nearby at all, the relax chip reads "All cuisines · N results" and tapping it switches to the All tab.
  • Relax. One suggestion per active filter, with the others held. Budget is a slider, so it suggests the lowest whole-dollar budget that gives at least one result ($16.32 rounds up to $17). Time and distance move to the next step that gives a result. If no single change helps, offer "Remove distance" or "Remove time". Tapping a chip applies it, moves the slider or step to match, and shows the new results.
  • Copy. Title "No exact matches". Body "Nothing fits a $15 budget, 15 min and 0.5 mi. Here are the closest meals." No apology, no dead end.

Backend

API

All read-only GET calls with query strings. Easy to test in a browser and easy to show in slides. Everything new lives under /api/quick-meal/. Existing restaurant and menu APIs stay as they are; Quick Meal reads their data and adds the estimated all-in prices on top. Other screens never see a change.

RouteReturns
GET /api/homeCategories, banner, cuisine chips, "Near campus" row.
GET /api/quick-meal/searchParams budget, time, distance, cuisine (one value, empty for All), sort. Returns exact (one lead meal per place, with its other fitting meals), near, relax, and total. It runs when the user applies filters or taps a tab.
GET /api/quick-meal/restaurants/:id?budget=One restaurant and its full menu in budget view. Each item has its est. all-in price, the breakdown lines, and, when a budget is given, "fits" or how much it is over.

For the presentation

Demo script, about 2 minutes

  1. Home. Tap Quick Meal.
  2. Tap Budget ▾. Slide to $25 and apply: 18 places have a meal that fits. Open it again, slide to $20 and apply: 10 places.
